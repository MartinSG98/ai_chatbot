You: What is photosynthesis?
Wolf: 📚 Researching...
Wolf: Photosynthesis is a biological process used by photopigment-bearing autotrophic organisms like most plants, algae, and cyanobacteria to convert light energy, usually from sunlight, into chemical energy. This chemical energy fuels their metabolism. The most common form, oxygenic photosynthesis, releases oxygen as a byproduct by splitting water molecules.

Photosynthetic organisms store this chemical energy in organic compounds such as carbohydrates (e.g., sugars like glucose, fructose, and sucrose). When the organism needs energy, it metabolizes these compounds through cellular respiration.

Photosynthesis is crucial because it maintains the oxygen content of Earth's atmosphere and supplies most of the biological energy necessary for complex life.

The process starts when light energy is absorbed by pigments like chlorophyll in plants, which are found in chloroplasts. This energy is used to strip electrons from substances such as water, producing oxygen gas and creating molecules like NADPH and ATP that store energy. Then, through a series of light-independent reactions called the Calvin cycle, atmospheric carbon dioxide is incorporated into sugars.

There are also other types of photosynthesis, such as anoxygenic photosynthesis found in some bacteria, which do not produce oxygen but instead release sulfur or use other mechanisms to generate energy.
